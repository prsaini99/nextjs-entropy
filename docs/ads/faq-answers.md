# MarTech FAQ — final copy

Answers written around the real USP: **AI-native delivery — fast and
inexpensive.** Typical project 2–3 weeks; smallest CRM and custom builds have
landed around $500.

Every capability claimed below is one that already exists in the product pages.
Nothing is invented. Where a claim couldn't be verified in the codebase it has
been softened or cut — see "Deliberately not claimed" at the end.

## How the USP is used

**Speed and price are the answer to almost every objection**, so they appear
across pages rather than in one "pricing" FAQ:

- *"custom takes too long"* → 2–3 weeks, not quarters
- *"custom is expensive"* → builds from ~$500; compare against a recurring licence
- *"we'd need a platform team"* → managed retainer
- *"why you over SaaS"* → you own it, and it costs less than a year of seats

**On quoting $500:** never as a headline price — it anchors too low and attracts
bargain hunters. Always as *evidence of efficiency* and always beside the range,
so an enterprise buyer doesn't disqualify you: "from around $500 for a focused
build up to full platform work."

## `/martech` — hub

**What does an AI marketing agency actually do?**
We build the marketing systems you'd otherwise rent — automation, influencer
platforms, calling agents, lead engines — and run campaigns on them. Most
agencies configure off-the-shelf SaaS and have never shipped software. Most dev
shops have never run a campaign. Doing both is why the tools fit how marketing
actually works.

**How is AI-native delivery different from a normal agency?**
We use AI through our own engineering — scoping, code, QA, documentation — with
senior engineers reviewing everything. That's why work priced and scheduled like
a quarter-long project ships in weeks. It's not a smaller version of the old
process; it's a different cost structure.

**How long does a martech build take?**
Most projects go live in **2–3 weeks**. Larger platform builds run longer and
ship in weekly increments, so you see working software early rather than waiting
for a big reveal. The 3–6 month timeline that made "building takes too long"
true in 2020 no longer applies.

**How much does it cost to build custom marketing software?**
Far less than most teams expect. Focused builds — a CRM, an internal tool, a
single automation — have started around **$500**, with full platform work priced
accordingly. The comparison that matters is three-year total cost: licences
recur and rise; a build stops costing you once it's yours.

**Build vs buy: when does custom marketing automation beat SaaS?**
Sooner than it used to. Per-seat and per-contact pricing scales with your
headcount and list size rather than your results, so the crossover often arrives
inside the first year — especially now that building takes weeks rather than
quarters. If your tool fits and the price doesn't grow, keep it. Most teams find
neither is true.

**Who maintains it after launch?**
We do, on a managed retainer — monitoring, API version upgrades and new
capabilities. Platform APIs change constantly and connectors rot without
maintenance. You don't need an in-house platform team to own your stack.

**What is a stack audit?**
We map your current tools, spend and data flows, find the shelfware and
integration debt, and tell you what to keep, replace and build — sequenced by
ROI. It's free, and it's useful even if you never build anything with us.

## `/martech/ai-call-center`

**What is an AI calling agent?**
A voice AI that answers and makes calls in real time — qualifying leads, booking
meetings, following up — and logs every transcript and outcome to your CRM.
Unlike an IVR it holds a real conversation: it handles interruptions and
understands intent instead of menu presses.

**What's the difference between an AI receptionist and an AI call center?**
A receptionist handles inbound — routing, messages, bookings. A call centre adds
outbound: follow-up calls, qualification, re-engaging leads that went quiet.
Ours does both from a single configuration, so you're not buying two systems.

**How much does an AI calling agent cost?**
Less than most teams assume, because a new company's agent is configured from
one profile rather than built from scratch. Compare it to a human calling team:
24/7 coverage, no shift scheduling, no attrition, no ramp time. We'll model it
against your actual call volumes in the first call.

**Can it handle Hindi and English in the same call?**
Yes — 11 languages in real time, including switching mid-conversation, which is
how most Indian customers actually speak. Agents locked to one language break on
the first switch. That's the specific case this was built for.

**What happens when the AI can't answer?**
It hands off to a human with the full conversation context, so the customer
never repeats themselves. Escalation rules are yours — by topic, by sentiment,
or whenever the caller asks for a person.

**How quickly can we go live?**
Days, not months. A new agent is configured from one profile with no redeploy,
so the time goes into your scripts, objection handling and escalation rules —
not engineering.

## `/martech/influencer-marketing`

**What's the difference between an influencer marketing platform and an agency?**
A platform gives you tools; an agency runs the campaign. We do both — Zyflus is
the software, and we've run creator campaigns on it. Most platform vendors have
never executed a campaign with their own product, which is why so many of them
optimise for demos rather than outcomes.

**How do you find the right influencers for a brand?**
Discovery pulls creators from Instagram, then AI scores each one 0–100 against
your ideal influencer profile. Your team reviews the top decile instead of
scrolling through thousands — the shortlist arrives already ranked.

**Can agencies manage multiple brands in one account?**
Yes. Multi-brand isolation is built in, so each client's creators, campaigns and
negotiations stay separate. It was designed for agency use rather than
retrofitted onto a single-brand tool.

**Do you run the campaigns or only provide the tool?**
Either. Some clients license Zyflus and run it themselves; others hand us the
campaign end to end. The software doesn't change — only who operates it.

**How does outreach automation avoid looking like spam?**
Sequences are personalised per creator and stop the moment someone replies. The
goal is starting more human conversations, not sending more DMs — a warm creator
should never go cold because someone forgot to follow up.

**Can we get a version customised to how we work?**
Yes, and that's usually the point. Zyflus is ours, so we can extend it around
your workflow rather than asking you to adapt to a roadmap. Because delivery is
AI-accelerated, that customisation is typically weeks, not quarters.

## `/martech/social-automation`

**What is Instagram DM automation?**
Software that replies to DMs and comments automatically — answering common
questions, qualifying interest, following up — so nobody waits hours for a
reply. Humans only handle the conversations that need judgement.

**Is Instagram DM automation allowed by Meta?**
Yes, within Meta's platform rules — which is why we build on the official
Instagram Graph API rather than browser scripts that put your account at risk.
Automation should build your brand, not gamble it.

**Can automated replies sound like my brand?**
Yes — replies are drafted in your brand voice, and you can keep a human review
step before anything sends. Anything the bot shouldn't answer gets routed to a
person instead of guessed at.

**What's the difference between a chatbot and DM automation?**
A chatbot answers when spoken to. DM automation also initiates, follows up on a
schedule, stops the moment someone replies, and routes qualified conversations
to a human. It's a pipeline, not a widget.

**How fast do replies go out?**
Under a minute, around the clock. On social, that's frequently the whole
difference between your reply and a competitor's.

**Can you scrape data we need that isn't on social?**
Yes — competitors, pricing, prospects, trends, from any public source, on a
schedule, into your warehouse. Most of these are small, focused builds, which is
why they're often the fastest and cheapest thing we do.

## `/martech/lead-intelligence`

**How do you build a B2B lead generation system?**
Three parts: a data engine that finds and enriches prospects, a CRM that
captures every WhatsApp, call and email in one timeline, and AI scoring that
tells reps who to call next and what to say. Most teams have the first two and
are missing the third.

**Is web scraping for lead generation legal in India?**
Gathering publicly available business information is generally permissible; how
you store and use personal data is what the DPDP Act governs. We build access
controls and retention rules in from the start rather than bolting them on
afterwards.

**How is this different from Apollo or ZoomInfo?**
Those are subscriptions to someone else's database, priced per seat and per
credit. This is your own pipeline and your own schema, in your own warehouse —
enriched continuously, and it doesn't get more expensive as your team grows.

**What is AI lead scoring?**
A model that reads every interaction — calls, WhatsApp threads, emails — and
assigns each lead a score, a temperature and a next action. Reps open the CRM
already knowing who to call.

**Can you build a CRM around how we actually sell?**
Yes, and this is one of the cheapest things we do. Focused CRM builds have
started around **$500** — usually less than a year of seats on an off-the-shelf
tool that still doesn't fit. We run our own BD on the CRM we built ourselves.

## `/martech/ai-integration`

**What is MCP (Model Context Protocol)?**
An open standard for connecting AI assistants to real systems as tools. Instead
of copy-pasting between ChatGPT and ten dashboards, your AI queries your ad
accounts, CRM, email and analytics directly.

**How do I connect my CRM to ChatGPT or Claude?**
Through a custom MCP connector that exposes the actions you want and withholds
the ones you don't. We map your platforms, workflows and business rules first —
an AI is only as useful as its understanding of how you operate.

**Is it safe to let AI act on our marketing accounts?**
That's what the approval guardrails are for. High-consequence actions — spend
changes, sends, publishing — pass through a human gate. The AI drafts; a person
confirms.

**What does MCP integration cost, and how long does it take?**
Individual connectors are small, fast pieces of work — typically days to a
couple of weeks each. We usually pair them with a retainer, because platform
APIs change and unmaintained connectors quietly stop working.

## `/martech/proposal-maker`

**What is an AI proposal generator?**
Software that drafts a proposal from your previous mandates, applies your brand
design automatically, recommends the quote from your pricing history — and then
tells you which sections the client actually read.

**Can it use my brand's design?**
Yes — your logo, type and colours on every proposal, generated on brand by
default rather than a stock template with a logo dropped into the corner.

**How is this different from PandaDoc or Proposify?**
Those are documents you fill in. This drafts the content from your own won
proposals, recommends pricing from your history, and suggests ideas the client
didn't ask for. And it's yours outright, not a per-seat subscription that grows
with your team.

**How do reading analytics help close deals?**
You see which sections were read and for how long, so follow-ups are timed by
the client's behaviour rather than your memory — and pipeline reporting reflects
real engagement instead of optimism.

## Deliberately not claimed

Cut or softened because the product pages don't support them:

- **Nothing quantified that isn't already on the site.** No invented uptime,
  accuracy or conversion figures.
- **"Consent management"** for the scraper stack softened to access controls and
  retention rules — `Compliance-Aware by Design` is the strongest claim the page
  makes.
- **No named-competitor benchmarks.** "Different from Apollo/PandaDoc" describes
  the *model* (subscription vs owned), never their performance.
- **No specific pricing table.** $500 appears only as a floor with range beside
  it, on two pages, as evidence of efficiency.
- **DPDP wording is deliberately careful** — "generally permissible" and "what
  the Act governs", not a legal opinion.

## Two site changes this creates

**1. `MartechProcess.jsx` contradicts the USP.** It says "From Tool Sprawl to
Owned Stack in **90 Days**" with "Build & Integrate — **Week 4–12**", and the
hero says "First Release in 6-12 Weeks". If most projects finish in 2–3 weeks,
this copy is losing you deals against faster-sounding competitors. Either the
timeline is wrong or 2–3 weeks describes only smaller builds — worth settling,
because speed is your strongest differentiator and the site currently hides it.

**2. The budget dropdown excludes your best segment.** Lowest option is
**"Under ₹5 Lakh"** (~$6,000) but you've delivered from ~$500 (~₹42,000). A
prospect with a ₹50,000 budget reads that list, decides they're too small, and
leaves. Suggested replacement:

```
Under ₹1 Lakh
₹1 – ₹5 Lakh
₹5 – ₹15 Lakh
₹15 – ₹40 Lakh
₹40 Lakh+
Not sure yet
```

Note this also changes the ad strategy: at that price point your addressable
market is far larger than enterprise martech, and the SMB and startup buyer is
suddenly in scope.
